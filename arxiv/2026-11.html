cond-mat.supr-concond-mat.str-el

The predominantly $d_{x^2-y^2}$-wave pairing symmetry of cuprate superconductors is well established, yet its microscopic manifestation in local Cooper-pair tunneling remains less explored. Here, we investigate Bi$_{1.2}$Pb$_{0.8}$Sr$_2$CaCu$_2$O$_{8+δ}$, using Josephson scanning tunneling spectroscopy with a conventional $s$-wave superconducting Pb tip. We observe a zero-bias conductance enhancement whose approximately quadratic dependence on junction conductance is consistent with phase-diffusive Josephson tunneling. Atomic-resolution conductance maps and site-resolved spectra reveal a pronounced intra-unit-cell modulation, with enhanced Josephson response near oxygen sites and strong suppression near copper sites and along diagonal directions. Microscopic calculations incorporating the bilayer electronic structure and realistic Wannier-based tunneling matrix elements reproduce the observed spatial pattern through interference between pairing contributions of opposite signs. Our results reveal how the sign-changing $d$-wave superconducting order parameter manifests itself in atomic-scale Josephson tunneling, providing complementary evidence for unconventional pairing symmetry in cuprates.

Interplay between Spin Stripes and Pairing Crossover in the Bilayer Hubbard Model

Zixuan Jia, Xuelei Sui, Shijie Hu, Haiqing Lin, Bing Huang Zixuan Jia Xuelei Sui Shijie Hu Tianxing Ma Haiqing Lin Bing Huang

cond-mat.str-el

Whether $d$-wave or $s_\pm$-wave pairing prevails in the bilayer nickelate \ce{La3Ni2O7} remains unresolved, and how its observed spin order and magnetic excitations relate to the pairing mechanism is unclear. Here, we use finite-temperature determinant quantum Monte Carlo simulations of a minimal bilayer Hubbard model to investigate pairing competition at strong interlayer coupling, where intralayer and interlayer antiferromagnetic correlations have comparable strengths. We find that spin stripes develop spontaneously with increasing hole doping, accompanied by a crossover of the dominant pairing symmetry from $d$-wave to $s_\pm$-wave. The crossover tracks the changing relative strengths of the spin correlations, with the interlayer correlations overtaking their intralayer counterparts in magnitude. Concomitantly, the dynamical spin structure factor exhibits a momentum-dependent reorganization of the magnetic excitations, consistent with fluctuating spin stripes. Our results identify the structure of spin fluctuations as a key link between spin stripes and pairing competition, offering a minimal-model perspective for understanding \ce{La3Ni2O7}.

Rare-earth nickelates are among the most active oxide electrocatalysts for the oxygen evolution reaction (OER), owing to their tunable Ni-O covalency, near-unity eg occupancy, and sensitivity to A-site chemistry, oxygen stoichiometry, and epitaxial strain. Here, we use epitaxial A-site high-entropy (La0.2Pr0.2Nd0.2Sm0.2Eu0.2)NiO3 thin films to reveal how epitaxial strain influences defect formation, Ni valence, electronic structure, and OER activity in a chemically disordered rare-earth (RE) sublattice. Tensile strain promotes Ruddlesden-Popper fault formation, interrupts the corner-sharing NiO6 network, and lowers the average Ni oxidation state. Although the occupied density of states near the Fermi level remains largely unchanged, O K-edge X-ray absorption spectroscopy reveals increased low-energy unoccupied states under tensile strain. These coupled structural and electronic changes lead to earlier OER onset, stronger pre-catalytic Ni redox features, and higher activity than compressively strained films and representative single-A-site nickelates. The high-entropy nickelate also shows enhanced resistance to OER-induced structural degradation compared with LaNiO3. These results suggest that A-site configurational disorder and epitaxial strain can jointly tune defect formation and rare-earth-derived electronic states, providing a route for improving perovskite oxide electrocatalysts.

cond-mat.str-elcond-mat.quant-gas

We show that the boundary fractionalization familiar from gapped topological insulators can enforce bulk gaplessness in a dipole-conserving system. In a one-dimensional fermion model with charge conservation, dipole conservation, and particle-hole symmetry, we realize an intrinsically gapless symmetry-protected topological (igSPT) phase combining a dipolar Luttinger liquid with half-quantized boundary charges. This boundary structure obstructs a unique gapped bulk preserving these symmetries and lattice translation. Remarkably, the two charge-neutral edge configurations remain exactly degenerate even at finite size due to dipole conservation and particle-hole symmetry. The boundary fractionalization also manifests as shifted conformal towers in the dipole-resolved entanglement spectrum, reflecting distinct conformal boundary conditions of the Luttinger liquid. We support our analysis of the igSPT by studying exactly-solvable limits and performing DMRG computations, establishing its stability for a wide range of tuning parameters.

Quantum impurity calculations become challenging when multiple correlated orbitals are treated explicitly, particularly in nonequilibrium steady states. We introduce Coupled-Cluster Coupled Lindblad (C$^3$L), a deterministic approach that combines the recently developed coupled-Lindblad compact bath representation with coupled-cluster theory to compute steady states and Green's functions directly in imaginary and real frequency. C$^3$L has favorable polynomial computational complexity and avoids the sign problem of quantum Monte Carlo (QMC). In a real-time multiorbital benchmark, it agrees closely with state-of-the-art QMC while reducing computational cost by four to five orders of magnitude, with a further 10--100-fold speedup on a GPU. Beyond the practical reach of established real-time QMC solvers, four-orbital nonequilibrium impurities with general interactions and biased reservoirs become accessible with C$^3$L. We obtain resonance-resolved transport signatures relevant to scanning tunneling microscopy experiments on molecular junctions.

Establishing Néel order in the square-lattice antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model: a bootstrap proof

arXiv:2611.03241v1 Published: 2026-11-02 LLM Score: 0.950 dmrg_preprints
cond-mat.stat-mech

Rigorously establishing spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) in the thermodynamic limit is generally challenging. This is exemplified by the two-dimensional (2D) $S = 1/2$ antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model where the existence of Néel order is not rigorously established despite overwhelming evidence from a variety of methods. We show that a bootstrap approach can be used to rigorously establish SSB and use it to prove the existence of Néel order in the 2D $S = 1/2$ XXZ model by deriving a strictly positive lower bound on the Néel order parameter for a set of discrete values of the anisotropy parameter $Δ$ in the range $[0,2]$ including the isotropic (Heisenberg) point $Δ= 1$. Our approach employs a generalization of the Kennedy--Lieb--Shastry inequalities, which use an infrared bound to reduce the existence of long-range order to bounds on certain finite-distance correlators. We compute these bounds for all sufficiently large systems with periodic boundary conditions using the bootstrap. An important ingredient in our proof is a novel method for obtaining exactly certified variational upper bounds on the energy density in the thermodynamic limit from matrix product states. Our approach can be readily generalized to other local lattice models for which infrared susceptibility bounds can be obtained.

Sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD) accuracy remains limited for strongly correlated systems like iron-sulfur clusters. This accuracy heavily depends on the mean-field reference state, which determines the sampling ansatz and configuration-recovery target. We extend the qcsc-prefect workflow to support a broken-symmetry unrestricted Hartree-Fock (BS-UHF) starting point, derived from atom-localized antiferromagnetic initial guesses. We conclude that reference-state preparation, rather than subspace size alone, is a key driver of SQD energy accuracy under strong electronic correlation.

cond-mat.stat-mech

We propose a weakly coupled description of the long-range transverse-field Ising chain near its short-range crossover at $s_*=7/4$, where the couplings decay as $r^{-1-s}$. The Ising conformal field theory is coupled to an instantaneous Gaussian field through $σχ$, extending the classical long-range Ising duality to quantum chains. The equal-time interaction generates a stress-tensor deformation that causes the velocity to run and renormalizes the dynamical critical exponent $z$. Conformal perturbation theory gives $z=1-0.720\,δ+O(δ^2)$, $1/ν=(1+z)/2+O(δ^2)$, and $ω=δ+O(δ^2)$, with $δ=s_*-s$. DMRG calculations on periodic chains of up to $128$ sites provide a consistency test using theoretical inputs for $1/ν$ and $ω$. After accounting for slow finite-size corrections, static and dynamical estimates of $z$ agree, supporting the crossover expansion at $s=1.5$ and a two-sided Padé interpolation at $s=1$, $1.25$, and $1.5$.

cond-mat.str-el

Many-body physics faces an apparent paradox concerning violations of Luttinger's theorem in strongly correlated systems. The theorem relates the electron number to the Luttinger count -- the monodromy of the logarithm of the imaginary-frequency Green's function $G(iε)$ -- and, at finite magnetic field, connects the Hall conductivity to the magnetic-field derivative of the Luttinger count via the Streda formula. Here, we provide a non-perturbative solution to this paradox. We demonstrate that the reported violations of Luttinger's theorem do not require a breakdown of the skeleton diagram representation of physical quantities, thereby resolving a long-standing point of contention in many-body theory. Furthermore, we show that the Luttinger integral, which accounts for the mismatch between the particle number and the Luttinger count, is itself a genuine topological invariant directly tied to the Luttinger-Ward functional $Φ[G]$ rather than to the Green's function alone. We demonstrate that this invariant, like the monodromy of $\ln G(iε)$ itself, stems exclusively from the zero-frequency discontinuity of the Matsubara trajectory.

The spin-1/2 kagome Heisenberg antiferromagnet is a central model in the study of quantum spin liquids, yet its ground-state properties remain unresolved. We perform large-scale variational Monte-Carlo calculations using neural-network quantum states (NQSs) with kagome-geometry-aware message-passing convolution on two-dimensional tori containing up to 300 spins, and achieve exceptionally low variational energies for the kagome model. The spin and dimer structure factors show no evidence of magnetic or valence-bond long-range order. Together, these complementary results provide evidence for a $U(1)$ Dirac spin-liquid ground state in the kagome Heisenberg antiferromagnet.

We present a first-principles study of the formal-charge polar discontinuity in superlattices of BaTiO$_3$ interfaced with SmNiO$_3$ or BiNiO$_3$, comparing the results for the insulating and metallic nickelate phases. The complex-temperature phase structure of the two-dimensional Potts model has remained largely unresolved despite decades of study. Here we determine the complex-temperature phase diagram of the 3-state Potts model, and uncover three gapless phases beyond the familiar ferromagnetic and paramagnetic phases. This is done by combining tensor-network free energies with the Poisson equation to compute the Fisher zero density in the thermodynamic limit. The resulting zero distribution unifies and revises long-standing conjectures on the zero loci, and outlines each phase. One is a non-conformal gapless phase, with an exponentially growing number of low-lying transfer-matrix levels distributed across all momentum sectors. At the antiferromagnetic critical point, the Luttinger parameter equals 3, while its limit from the adjacent Luttinger-liquid phase is 6. These findings reveal rich phase structures and unconventional critical behavior in a classical textbook model.

There, a single Trotter step already requires two-qubit error rates of order 10^{-6}, beyond the reach of noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) hardware. We test the assumptions of this estimate with an imaginary-time matrix product state (MPS) that applies each dot as one block. It agrees with exact diagonalisation at (p, q) = (6, 2). At (5, 2), a variational circuit with the same dot-plus-link layout comes within 3.6% of the exact energy, and noisy simulations show that its fidelity decays as assumed in the resource estimate. At (8, 5), i.e., N = 40 qubits, the energy converges at bond dimension chi = 768, far below the exact value 2^20, and the inter-dot entanglement stays small and is set mainly by K. Out-of-time-order correlators across the links decay on a time scale that shrinks rapidly with |K|. Thus, the dot size p and the inter-dot coupling K together set the entanglement of the chain, and with it where a quantum advantage could appear.
